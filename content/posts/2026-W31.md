    - I had some great pairing sessions for setting up our new CI, and I found and resolved some pretty major bugs. I sure don't love the quality of code that AI tosses out, especially when it causes us to get locked out of boxes. But now I am in a position where I am cleaning up after this.

- Stylus & UserStyles blew up.
    - A bug caused my Stylus to be filled with nearly two thousand corrupted style entries with no size. Since there is no bulk delete, I was upset having to clear it out manually.
    - I decided to go back to having a git repo with all of my styles and scripts and then syncing them into Stylus & ViolentMonkey. This was something I experimented with before I had a publicly accessible forge, and it didn't work well. Now that I have Forgejo, it works great without other sync.
- Fridge Died
    - FUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUUCK
    - Monday I noticed the side of my fridge door was hot. The edge between the doors was venting a tonne of heat. I wrote this off as fridge doing fridge things for the day since I had infusions to get to, but then that evening I went to grab something from the freezer, and everything inside was completely melted. That's when I noticed all my frozen fruit was lightly chilled, gross liquid fruit. Again with the long drawn out fuck.
    - Sadly, the freezer just kept getting warmer, so it was time to do the research and look at fridges asap. I spent way more than I have to get it, but it's a very nice ranked "not smart" model and I was able to play with it at a store to explore it and make sure it will spark joy.
    - The new fridge definitely has a smaller freezer, but not problematically so for my needs, and it's so much easier to access things in all sections. The vegetable drawer is the best though. All my veggies cold and crisp, the ethylene absorbers, the humidity. That extremely sparks joy for me.
    - I was tempted to try and stall such a major purchase, but I don't have the immune system to play with maybe spoiled food.
- Making Caddy and Anubis play nice

    - Buy software, especially if there is somebody else giving it to you for free.
    - I used Kagi's Orion on Mac and missed it, but now I have Brave Origin. I want people to see foundational products we were getting for free for too long as a viable production. Plus they are doing perpetual, which I doubt is the most viable these days, but it's good for _me_.

I think it is odd that "make the internet broken and manually fix all the pages I use" is the part I miss and want back the most.

    - My eyes have not been doing great, though all the chemo, which has led me to start enjoying more assistive things on my phone, including having it read messages and dictating things to it.
    - Now I am trying to bring it to my Linux boxes with voxtype. Once I got it typing automatically and using the correct graphics card per machine, it's a great experience. I can't say it's faster than dictating on my phone, but it's great.

- Miniflux experiment
    - I have been using a struggling FreshRSS instance hosted by a friend since I left NewsBlur, and I decided to strike out and run my own Miniflux.
    - My first opinion is that it is fast, fast, fast, fast. It's also very pretty in that less-is-more way. I joked it looks like 2010s-era AdSense ads. But it has delightful little touches like the entries have a quick fade as you navigate through them.
    - The app looks great and navigates well on mobile. It doesn't navigate as well on mobile as it does on desktop for me since the whole thing is designed to be navigated with vim keys, another huge win for me personally.
    - I made a few small CSS tweaks to it to improve the typeface choices, but those are highly personal and were made easy by having a full set of CSS variables set up.
    - It has some annoying UX problems that I am not sure how to resolve. One is that you can't go back when reading unread articles, since it instantly marks them as read and the queue is live-updated in the background.
    - The documentation goes on large tirades about how opinionated it is. Since all software is opinionated, this usually means the dev only cares about their use case, and therefore PRs are not welcome. This doesn't bother me really; not all software that is a gift to the commons _also_ has to cater to the many random whims of the commons.
