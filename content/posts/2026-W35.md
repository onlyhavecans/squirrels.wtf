## Summary

- Starting the week with a failed hard drive
    - My Sunday stream was ended abruptly by a disk blowing out. It's a bummer, but something I can recover from pretty quickly with how I have my stack set up.
    - NixOS+dotfiles for rapid system & configuration deployment, plus data sync and 3-2-1 backups w/ vetted restores all came together to have my back. It took longer to swap the disk than to deal with the fallout.
    - The reason swapping took so long was a comedy of errors, all of my own making. I hadn't marked the disk as optional in fstab so that the system wouldn't boot without it. However, my systems are locked down and inaccessible on boot failure. I completely locked out the root user and emergency boot for security reasons, so I had to scramble for another computer to build an emergency boot disk so I could format the new disk, label the partition, and then boot into my system normally.
- OBS tweaks
    - I figured out more PipeWire tricks. This one allows me to null-sink audio I only want to hear through OBS. WirePlumber and PipeWire are pretty awesome for audio routing. I don't miss ALSA or Pulse at all.
- The pains of open source sometimes
    - An app I use was "a release away" from fixing most of my issues. But the maintenance and work on it have gone dead as of June. Noooooo. Forktime.
- Week 3 of being back on GLP-1
    - I don't love the side effects of the GLP-1, but I can't deny its value to me. I was taking enough insulin every day that I was having issues from it. And now that I am back on my GLP-1, I take no insulin at all, and my glucose is even lower. It's a great diabetes manager.
- The Remission Times
    - Since I shared the news last Friday (when I got the news officially), suddenly all my friends are crawling out of the woodwork and wanting to chat and hang out.
    - Sadly, my mental health is still low AF, and my energy level is worse. Like I am hypothetically excited for this, and glad everybody else is too, but I am also just so tired.
- Surprise Chemo!
    - You'd think I am in remission so that I would have less hospital time, but nooooooooooo. Four this week. MWThF for me, I guess. Daymn
    - And another surprise IT Chemo! Oh yay! This one really sucked because they had to give me double lumbar punctures since the first one wasn't getting pressure.
    - Then on the way home from all that chemo, I got a call from doctors letting me know I'm moving up CAR-T fast and chemos back off… after chemo. Sigh
    - I keep forgetting how hard the chemo to the spine hits. I had a 9am clinic visit the next day, and it was brutal. Now I am down hard with just that chemo kicking my butt.
- Planning for Utah Part 2
    - It has begun
- The end of the brave-origin experiment
    - It was fun, but not for me. I will probably keep it around, but Manifest v2 is kinda a deal breaker. I miss uBlockOrigin and others too much, and honestly I didn't notice a performance difference.
- The Shell Wars, Noctalia vs DankMaterialShell
    - MOAR CHANGES
    - Honestly, while I like a lot of the tooling I have set up, waybar has been a big struggle to get it the way I like; something where I am constantly tweaking this, that, or the other. Over and over, and it's never quite right.
    - I figured a way of switching into DMS without overhauling everything. By not running the setup, I can keep my normal launcher and setup and then walk myself back into using more and more of it. My previous experience was a bit painful because the "start from their defaults" didn't fly for me at all. However, since I last touched it, they have improved performance a lot, and honestly it's pretty nice to click to configure a bunch of things.
    - I still ended up struggling a bit with DMS. It didn't click with me, so I installed a few others and gave them a try.
    - The one that really stuck with me is Noctalia Shell v5. It's really fast and has amazing TOML configuration layering so that I can keep all my defaults in my dotfiles. Still, each system has its own overrides that are "click to undo"; this alone was super appealing so I could mess it all up and then undo a bunch of stuff. The bar has all the little swag and integrations I've wanted.
    - SO YES. Still tweaking my shell bunches. But this should cut down on a lot of that
- The death of a fan
    - It's amusing that a single fan failure can deactivate all of my systems. I mean, it was the CPU fan after all. Still, it required a replacement. I put a temporary BIOS override in and replaced the 95mm 12v DC fan with a 381mm 120v AC one while waiting for another new one to show up.
    - This was another test of how helpful having my KVM and ATX power control was. No way I could have troubleshot this from any distance otherwise.
