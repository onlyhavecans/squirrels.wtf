## Summary

- Streams
    - Sunday: Big fun plus a bit of collaboration.
    - Saturday: Finally got my couch test stream in!
    - It took more than a day of fiddling and swapping so many cables and adjusting, but I managed to get my streaming setup on my laptop and mobile enough to work even hanging out on a couch. There are some issues with apps that hate being logged in twice. Like logging into Twitch from Firebot on my laptop breaks auth from Firebot on my Desktop. Brutal.
- Trying to make my own squirreltuber software
    - Instead of getting my blog post out Sunday, I obsessed over the idea of making my own giftuber software; open source, Linux-first, and worked by creating a browser source instead of screen capture.
    - Of course that's a bigger lift than I originally figured but also… it's doable.
    - My choice of Rust may not have been my best play since it's expensive in CI and compile heat once the app gets bigger, but the runtime is so shockingly slim, and that pays dividends when the app is actually being used.
    - I started using this as a test bed for how to do the "swarm of agents" thing better with Claude. It's not made this app better in any real way, and I might end up scrapping and redoing it from scratch myself because of this. But my way of doing AI development leveled up a lot. So did my token usage, though. WOW.
    - After a few scraps and attempts to better understand how to design this app well. I came out with something really usable. I used it for my entire test stream and even wrote a StreamController plugin so I could control it with the Stream Deck, all a huge success.
    - The editor is still a mess; AI will have to help my design skills. I also keep finding all the lil features I need to add to polish it. But it's a fun project to have.
- Redoing my static site deployer as well
    - I guess I am more in my "clearing tech debt from the hospital" mood. This is a "quick fix" for how I deploy my static sites internally, and by quick fix I mean re-designing how it works.
    - The new method is great
- Repo consolidation
    - More cleanup, this is about me making a quick template and updating all my Golang repos to a standard setup because I have a lot of them and the spray of inconsistency is annoying.

    - He makes the proposal that software should work; that's actually the theme of the conference he is at. However, he asks, "whom does the software serve?" He then proposes, "Computers should always serve the one directly interacting with them," and the crowd claps, but the result of that statement is interesting.
